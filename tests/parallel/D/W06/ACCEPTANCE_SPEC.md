## Scope, authority and prerequisites

Support owns cases, case events and resolution requests. Reviews owns verified reviews and moderation cases. Communications owns notifications, attempts, conversations and messages. Media owns private evidence; Billing owns actual refund decisions and money effects; A owns customer preferences and the privacy surface. Each data owner performs its approved privacy fulfillment. Admin and gateway own no business database.

Privacy **intake**, authorized **status**, manual fulfillment and automated export/deletion are distinct scopes. The published `privacyCoordinatingOwner` is null; Support coordination is only D's proposal. A's W06 fulfillment request and B's proposed W07 financial privacy timing require an explicit E/product decision on coordinator, scope and timing. Do not quietly defer required W06 fulfillment or add automation or a global delete because intake exists. A broader required scope remains blocked until actually fulfilled; missing retention, consent, fulfillment or legal-hold decisions remain prerequisites. Source-owned retained financial records are preserved under accepted policy.

Conversation membership ownership is also unresolved. D Communications membership and participant references are an A proposal; C provides current Booking/Dispatch relationships, and E provides private identity mapping. C/D requests are not an accepted membership contract. Tests require a published owner and authoritative current membership derivation before using these inputs.

## Current source limitations and INT-D-01

Support and Reviews schemas currently contain only `ServiceMarker`; Reviews `src/index.ts` still exports an empty skeleton. Communications has marker, `InboxMessage`, `ProbeNotification`; Reporting has marker, Inbox and `ProbeProjection`. These do not establish any task-required product records or workflows. The admin reference's review counts, dispute rows and notification sent/scheduled strings are samples; its modal save handler is simulated. Customer reference rating is explicitly local and unpublished.

INT-D-01 is a **static finding, not a reproduced real-database failure**. Both D Inbox stores catch errors over the whole transaction and classify every `P2002` or `23505` as `DUPLICATE` without identifying the Inbox constraint or checking the winning hash. Shared consumer ACKs duplicate but dead-letters conflict. This can conceal competing changed-byte identity and unrelated effect uniqueness failure. Existing integration Case C2 tests sequential altered replay; fake unit Inbox outcomes cannot prove the race. RHASH/CHASH cases require real transaction and broker controls, including legitimate same-byte positive controls.
